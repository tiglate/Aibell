2. LAYER II BIT ALLOCATION TABLES

Bit rates below are kbit/s PER CHANNEL: total bit rate divided by 1 for single channel, or by 2 for stereo, joint stereo, and dual channel.
For non-free-format MPEG-1, the complete selection rule for permitted combinations is:

Bit rate per channel             32 kHz   44.1 kHz   48 kHz
32, 48                           B.2d     B.2c       B.2c
56, 64, 80                       B.2a     B.2a       B.2a
96, 112, 128, 160, 192            B.2b     B.2b       B.2a
Free format                      B.2b     B.2b       B.2a

Free-format selection depends only on sampling frequency, not on a bit rate inferred from frame length. The mode/total-bit-rate restrictions in section 4 apply before the non-free-format table is selected. In particular, per-channel rates 16, 24, 28, and 40 resulting from two-channel totals 32, 48, 56, and 80 are not permitted MPEG-1 combinations. Single-channel totals 224, 256, 320, and 384 are not permitted. All three MPEG-1 sampling frequencies admit the valid bit-rate/mode combinations in section 4.

In each matrix the column numbers are unsigned allocation codes; entries are nlevels. Zero means no allocation and zero subband samples. A dash means that the column is outside the allocation field's range. With nbal = 0, no allocation field is transmitted; the zero in column 0 denotes the implied absence of allocation. Every representable allocation code for nbal > 0 is valid; there are no forbidden allocation codes in these five tables.
The sum of nbal is for one set of allocation fields across subbands, not necessarily the total allocation-bit count in a two-channel frame.

To obtain s''', first obtain the unsigned sample code, including degrouping when required. Use 2 bits for a 3-level sample, 3 bits for a 5-level sample, and 4 bits for a 9-level sample. For an ungrouped sample, use its transmitted codeword width. Invert only the most significant bit. Interpret the result as a fractional two's-complement number: the most significant bit has weight -1, followed by weights 1/2, 1/4, and so on. With unsigned sample code q and sample width m, the equivalent numerical relation is s''' = q / 2^(m-1) - 1. Do not invert the most significant bit of the entire grouped codeword.

For a grouped codeword with unsigned value w and nlevels = L, the three sample codes q0, q1, q2, in chronological order, satisfy:
w = q0 + L*q1 + L^2*q2, with 0 <= q0, q1, q2 < L.
Thus the first sample is the least-significant base-L digit, the second is the next digit, and the third is the most-significant base-L digit. The transmitted bits of the codeword are still read most significant bit first.

5. DECODING BEHAVIOUR

For Layer II joint stereo, the mode extension selects a nominal bound of 4, 8, 12, or 16 subbands for mode-extension values 00, 01, 10, or 11 respectively. The effective bound is the smaller of that value and sblimit. A bound greater than sblimit therefore gives independent channel allocation and sample coding throughout all transmitted subbands; no intensity-coded subband remains.

Below the effective bound, the two channels have separate allocation fields and separate coded samples. From the effective bound through sblimit-1, one allocation field and one set of coded samples are shared. The channels retain separate scalefactor selection information and scalefactors, so each channel multiplies the shared requantized samples by its own scalefactor.

All 36 subband samples per frame in every channel are zero for subband indices sblimit through 31 inclusive. This includes subband sblimit itself. The ranges are:
Table        Subbands forced to zero
3-B.2a       27..31
3-B.2b       30..31
3-B.2c        8..31
3-B.2d       12..31
LSF B.1      30..31
A subband below sblimit with allocation code zero also has zero samples. No allocation, scalefactor selection information, scalefactors, or sample codewords are transmitted for subbands at or above sblimit.

When error protection is present, the Layer II CRC covers, in bitstream order:
- Header bits 16 through 31 inclusive, counting the first syncword bit as bit 0: bit-rate index (4 bits), sampling-frequency index (2), padding (1), private bit (1), mode (2), mode extension (2), copyright (1), original/copy (1), and emphasis (2).
- Every transmitted allocation field below sblimit: both channels below the effective bound, and the single shared allocation above it in joint stereo.
- Every transmitted 2-bit scalefactor selection information field for a nonzero allocation. Both channels contribute their own selection information even where allocation and samples are shared.
The CRC excludes header bits 0..15, the transmitted CRC word itself, the scalefactor values, sample codewords, and ancillary data. It does not add byte-alignment bits at the end of the scalefactor selection information. The same Layer II coverage applies at the MPEG-2 low sampling frequencies.
